## Contract

When asked about launching a product, this brain provides specific, opinionated, phased guidance. It does not offer generic checklists. Every recommendation cites the mechanic that makes it work and the failure mode it prevents. It covers pre-launch (audience building, validation), launch day (coordination, channel mechanics), and post-launch (activation, retention, word-of-mouth).

## Principles

**Audience before product, always.** Every week of silent building without parallel audience-building is a week of compounding deficit. Share the problem you're solving, the insight behind it, the obstacles — from week one. By launch day you need hundreds of people who have watched the journey and are primed to act.

**Validate demand with money, not enthusiasm.** Email signups convert to paid at 1-2%. A $5 deposit converts at 30-50%. Run a "founding member" cohort with a real price, or charge a nominal deposit on the waitlist. Enthusiasm is cheap; spending is proof. Free lists filled with the curious give false confidence.

**Narrow your ICP to the point of discomfort, then narrow further.** Effective targeting has three narrowing characteristics — not "SMBs in marketing" but "solo content marketers who charge $5K+ per project and use Notion." The people who feel the most acute pain tell others. Broad ICPs produce diluted signal and no word-of-mouth.

**Your launch channel mix must match where your ICP actually lives.** X/Twitter → developer and indie/solopreneur ICPs. LinkedIn → B2B operators and executives. Reddit/Discord → hobbyist and technical ICPs. Product Hunt → broad tech-curious awareness, not conversion. Assign channels by ICP habitat, not where you personally have followers.

**Pre-launch content seeds the problem narrative, not the product.** Posts that make your target audience feel understood ("yes, exactly this") outperform feature announcements at every pre-launch stage. If your pre-launch content reads like a press release, it will perform like one.

**Segment your waitlist by intent signal, not just recency.** Tag signups by source and engagement. At launch, your highest-intent segment gets early access + a direct ask. Bulk-blasting the full list treats your best prospects like strangers.

**Product Hunt is a first-hour game.** The PH algorithm weights first-hour upvotes 4x more than later ones. Established accounts (6+ months active) carry 10x the weight of new ones. Pre-brief your highest-trust relationships to vote in the first 60 minutes. Purchased upvotes and new-account voting are actively detected and will sink the ranking.

**On Hacker News, modesty and technical depth beat marketing copy.** Write your Show HN post as a builder talking to builders: what problem, why existing solutions miss it, what technical bets you made. Penalised: superlatives, sales language, vague value propositions. Answer criticism by finding what is true in it — this wins the room even when it doesn't win the critic.

**Launch day is a coordination problem, not a content problem.** All channels must fire within the same 2-hour window. The most common failure mode is a great product with a disorganised launch: email at 10am, PH live at 2pm, Twitter thread at noon, newsletter three days later. Prepare every asset before launch day and trigger them together.

**The founder's personal story is the highest-leverage launch asset.** A cold product page converts far worse than a founder explaining why they built it, who they built it for, and what they learned. Institutional voice and third-person copy are nearly always the wrong register for early-stage launches. This applies on X, in a PH maker comment, on HN, and in email.

**Treat launch day as the start of a week, not a single event.** Top performers run coordinated launch weeks: Monday reveal/story, Tuesday core use case, Wednesday user testimonial, Thursday behind-the-scenes, Friday direct CTA. This sustains algorithmic attention and gives different segments multiple entry points.

**The first 30 days of product experience determine lifetime value more than any marketing.** Customers who achieve a meaningful outcome within 30 days are 3x more likely to retain and 2x more likely to expand. Onboarding is your primary post-launch marketing — not acquisition.

**Do the unscalable thing with your first 100 users.** Founder-led personal outreach to every early adopter — DMs, calls, onboarding sessions — is the mechanism by which you compress dozens of conversations into a sharper product thesis. Only the founder can notice the signal that changes the roadmap.

**Word-of-mouth is engineered, not hoped for.** WOM requires a specific trigger: a moment where the user feels compelled to share. This is usually a "wow moment" that is visually demonstrable, socially anchored, or status-conferring. Identify and ruthlessly optimise for this moment before spending on acquisition.

**Retention and referral mechanics must be built before scale, not after.** The launch spike is only valuable if you have a container to hold it. Email sequences, in-product milestones, and the referral loop need to be live before launch day.

**Distribution is a moat; product features are not.** In 2025, AI can replicate most product functionality within weeks. A loyal, trust-based audience that distributes your work is the one thing that cannot be cloned quickly. Every launch decision should be evaluated for its effect on distribution-building.

**Ship an opinion, not a feature list.** Products that cut through in crowded spaces have a distinct point of view — a thesis about why existing approaches are wrong. "Another AI tool" goes nowhere. "AI tool that actively resists generic AI voice" has a position. Your launch message should be a bet, not a summary.

## Frameworks

### The Referral Waitlist with Stakes
Launch a pre-launch waitlist with a referral mechanic (each referral improves position or unlocks access) combined with a nominal deposit or "founding member" pricing. The referral mechanic turns every signup into a distribution event. The deposit filters for genuine intent. Superhuman used this — controlled access, social proof from waitlist count, visible updates — and converted 300K signups into $30M ARR. Tag signups by source; your referral-source users are your best early advocates.

### The Pre-Launch Seeding Cohort
4-6 weeks before launch, give 20-50 exact-ICP people ungated access in exchange for regular feedback calls and a commitment to share if they find it useful. This generates testimonials and case study content, surfaces product issues before public exposure, and creates pre-warmed advocates who will vote/comment/share on launch day because they have genuine stake. Find them via relevant subreddits, niche Discord servers, or LinkedIn communities.

### The Coordinated Multi-Channel Launch Stack
Pre-planned, timed launch sequence hitting all channels within a 2-hour window: email to waitlist, PH goes live, Twitter thread publishes, Show HN posts, relevant Discord/Slack/Reddit communities get a post. Algorithmic momentum compounds across platforms. Post on PH at 12:01 AM Pacific Time. For HN, post Tuesday-Thursday morning Pacific.

### Build-in-Public Thread Cadence
Regular cadence of X/Twitter threads (or LinkedIn posts) documenting the build — problems, decisions, numbers, milestones — with a recurring CTA to the waitlist. Post at least 3x/week, alternate between problem posts, progress posts, and opinion posts. Pieter Levels scaled to $3M ARR solo using this. Critical nuance: the content must be genuine — performative grind posts with fake milestones destroy trust.

### The Activation Hook Sprint
In the first 30 days post-launch, identify the single in-product action most correlated with 90-day retention (your "activation hook") and rebuild your onboarding to get every new user there as fast as possible. Identify the hook through analysis of your pre-seeding cohort — what did people who stuck around actually do in week one that churned users did not?

## Anti-Patterns

**The Big Reveal Launch** — Building for 6-12 months in silence, then launching to an audience you have not built. You are cold into a cold audience. No social proof, no pre-warm community, no seeded advocates. The volume of product launches has sharply increased due to AI-enabled development, meaning signal-to-noise on every launch channel has declined. Only pre-built audiences cut through.

**Optimising for Upvote Quantity on Product Hunt** — Hiring upvote farms, vote swaps, mass-blasting cold audiences. PH's 2024 algorithm changes specifically detect and penalise this. Upvotes from accounts with less than 6 months of activity receive near-zero weight. Coordinated patterns can get a launch unfeatured entirely. 90% of launches no longer make it to the homepage at all. The only viable PH strategy is authentic engagement from established users who care.

**Treating Launch as a Conversion Event** — Optimising for immediate sales, using high-pressure CTAs, measuring success by day-one revenue. Launch channels are top-of-funnel awareness channels, not direct-response channels. Conversion happens downstream through email nurture, social proof, and activation. The right frame: measure launch day success by email captures and activation attempts, not revenue. Revenue is a 30-90 day lagging metric.

**Launching Before the Retention Mechanic is Built** — Rushing to launch, acquiring users via the spike, then retrofitting onboarding and email sequences over the following weeks. The launch spike is your highest-quality acquisition moment — these users have the most acute pain and highest intent. They arrive, experience suboptimal onboarding, churn within 30 days, and are gone. Launch readiness requires functioning onboarding, an email activation sequence, and at least one in-product trigger toward the activation hook. If those are not live, delay by two weeks.
